# Reading Pricing Feedback

Buyer feedback about price is one of the noisiest signals in a campaign. Here's how we read it.

## What buyers *say* vs what they *mean*

- "It's a bit out of our range" → could mean $20k or $200k; we always ask
- "We're looking at a few others" → usually a polite no on price specifically
- "We love it but…" → almost always followed by a price objection
- Silence after a second inspection → the strongest negative signal there is

## The 10 / 20 / 30 rule

Across hundreds of campaigns, a rough rule of thumb:

- **10 inspections, no offers** → price is in the right ballpark; keep going
- **20 inspections, no offers** → price is 5–10% above market; consider an adjustment
- **30 inspections, no offers** → price is more than 10% above market; act before the campaign goes stale

## Why we don't drop the price on day five

- Buyers compare your listing to its **history**, not just its current price
- Dropping early signals desperation and can attract lowball offers
- Two weeks of clean campaign data gives us a much stronger negotiating position

## What we'll show you when it's time

- Comparable sales from the last 90 days, within 1km
- Listings that started near your price and where they sold
- The shape of your enquiry curve vs the suburb average
